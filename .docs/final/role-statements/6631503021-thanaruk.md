## Statement (≈200 words)

I wrote the documents that define the problem and shape the solution: the proposal, the product backlog with
requirements `PB-01…PB-15` and their acceptance criteria, the compliance rules that constrain the build (PDPA,
Computer Crime Act §26, Electronic Transactions Act §9) mapped onto features, and the design draft with the use
case, activity, sequence and class diagrams. I also own the team's repository on GitHub.

My decision was to split check-in and check-out into separate requirements for the group cases rather than
leave them as one story. "Check in with an NFC tag" hides three different behaviours — open a new session, join
the group whose window is still open, or reopen an existing roster — and each one fails differently. Splitting
them into `PB-11`, `PB-12` and `PB-13` is why the test plan can trace a case to a specific group flow instead
of a vague one.

What I would do differently: I wrote the three pains without evidence behind them. They are now sourced in
`docs/planning/secondary-review.md` and in the owner conversation, but that should have happened before the
backlog was written, not after. Related: `PB-14` asks the staff view to name a table, and the data model never
had table identity — a requirement I wrote that the build could not satisfy.

Evidence: `docs/planning/backlog.md`, `docs/compliance/rule.md`, `docs/design/designdraft.md` §4.1–4.4.
